Written up the way Lectern writes up a lecture.

Recording

  • Two sources: the microphone in the room, or system audio from Zoom and your browser
    • macOS asks for recording permission first
  • Zoom reminders: with Canvas connected, Lectern finds Zoom invitations in your course messages and prompts you at class time
    • Recording starts only when you click

Transcripts and notes

  • English and mixed English–Hebrew: separate note layouts for lectures and shiurim
    • Check names and source references against the recording
  • Your own note style: give Lectern one to five examples of your notes, and future notes follow them
  • Your choice of AI: Antigravity by default, or ChatGPT through Codex, or OpenCode

Your semester

  • Canvas: your calendar, assignments, grades, and announcements in one place
    • Optional. You can manage your courses directly in Lectern
  • YU Torah subscriptions: new shiurim come into your library
  • Course chat: ask questions about the documents you pick for a course

Taking it elsewhere

  • Anki: sync a lecture's flashcards to a deck, or export a CSV
    • Download sample Anki cards
  • Google Docs: one doc for each course, one tab for each lecture
  • ChatGPT and Claude: share the courses you pick, then ask about them from either app

Where your lectures live

  • On your Mac: recordings, transcripts, and notes stay in your local library
  • What leaves, and when: only for the features you turn on
    • AI features send prompts and selected content to the provider you chose
    • Google Docs export sends notes to your Google account
    • ChatGPT and Claude sharing uploads the courses you select, and you can delete that copy
  • Read how your data is handled

Before you download

  • Q: What do I need?
    • A: An Apple silicon Mac (M1 or newer) on macOS 26 or later, and a Google account with Antigravity access
  • Q: Will macOS block it?
    • A: Yes, the first time. The current release isn't notarized by Apple, so you approve it once with Open Anyway. The installation guide shows each step
  • Q: Is it free?
    • A: Lectern is free and open source. The transcription and AI providers you set up may charge separately
  • Q: Do I need Canvas or Google Docs?
    • A: No. Both are optional
